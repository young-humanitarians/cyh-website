workshops:
- title: Taking the Law Out of the Book
  organisation: >-
    ICRC and the Committee for the Jean Pictet Competition – Marie-Louise Tougas and Ana Urgoiti
  format: In-person
  description: >-
    International Humanitarian Law plays a crucial role in regulating the conduct of hostilities
    and preserving a minimum of humanity during armed conflict. By limiting means and methods
    of warfare and protecting civilians and those hors de combat, it provides essential safeguards
    even in the most challenging circumstances. This simulation offers participants an immersive,
    practical introduction to the application of IHL through dynamic, scenariobased exercises.
    Building on the long-standing legacy of the JeanPictet Competition, it deepens participants’
    understanding of IHL’s principles and their relevance in realworld operational contexts.
- title: Improving Protection of Humanitarian Action in War Zones
  organisation: >-
    Dr. Daniel Trusilo, Collegium Fellow, University of St. Gallen, and former USAID Humanitarian
    Advisor
  format: In-person
  description: >-
    We are at an inflection point for humanitarian diplomacy and modern warfare. Namely, both
    the global humanitarian community and modern militaries have arrived at a point when a
    basic universal mechanism for humanitarian notification is feasible and necessary. Through
    a group-based simulation and discussion, this workshop will explore challenges and opportunities
    to improving humanitarian notification systems.
- title: >-
    Caught in the Crossfire? International NGOs and Contemporary Forms of Protest and Resistance
  organisation: Médecins Sans Frontières - Maelle L’Homme
  format: In-person
  description: >-
    On one hand, MSF has a history of very tangible relationships with social movements and
    has sometimes recruited individuals specifically for their activist status; on the other,
    there is also a form of initial reluctance to get involved in political dynamics that
    go beyond the organization’s scope of intervention. How do organizations like MSF establish
    roots within the societies or communities with or for whom they work? What does this imply?
    And what are the limits?
- title: Finding New Funding Avenues for the Humanitarian World
  organisation: Bublu Thakur-Weigold, INSEAD HRG
  format: In-person
  description: >-
    In this workshop, you will apply a proven innovation method to address a urgent problem
    faced by the humanitarian sector. Your team will be challenged to come up with new sources
    of funding for humanitarian aid, together with mechanisms beyond classic fundraising.
    Drawing on the collective intelligence and managerial experience of the group, you will
    evaluate the consequences of new pathways in pursuit of sustainable financial participation
    in global development and emergency response.
- title: 'Human Before Action: Turning stories into humanitarian impact'
  organisation: Waves to Home, Zoya Miari
  format: In-person
  description: >-
    What happens when humanitarian action begins with the human story? In this interactive
    workshop, participants will explore their personal story as a foundation for meaningful
    action. Through visual storytelling and collective reflection, we connect who we are,
    what shaped us, to the impact we want to create. This workshop invites you to move from
    story to action, without losing the human in humanitarianism.
- title: 'At the Frontline of Negotiation: A humanitarian simulation experience'
  organisation: Centre of Competence on Humanitarian Negotiations (CCHN)
  format: In-person
  description: >-
    Step into the world of high-stakes humanitarian negotiation through an immersive, hands-on
    simulation that mirrors real field realities. Participants will tackle complex access
    challenges, engage tough counterparts, and make critical decisions under pressure in a
    safe but demanding learning environment. Blending theory, role-play, and expert facilitation,
    the workshop sharpens negotiation instincts, strategic thinking, and de-escalation skills.
    It is a dynamic experience designed to shed the light on humanitarian challenges in volatile
    environment.
- title: >-
    Beyond the Headlines: What Europe’s external borders really look like & what you can do
    about it
  organisation: Arno Tanner, Europe Cares
  format: Online
  description: >-
    Arno Tanner is a humanitarian aid worker with several years of experience in medical and
    community-based projects at Europe’s external borders in Greece, as well as in search
    and rescue operations in the Central Mediterranean. In this workshop, he will share first-hand
    stories from camps, clinics, and rescue missions, showing what life is really like for
    people on the move and how the role of the Rule of Law is or is not applied. Participants
    will learn about the daily realities of border violence, restricted access to aid, and
    the challenges faced by those who try to help. Through these concrete examples, the session
    highlights the human impact of Europe’s borders beyond headlines. It concludes with practical
    ways for young people to get informed, engaged, and take meaningful action.
- title: >-
    Turning the Tide on Food Crises: Empowering youth for resilient futures through agriculture
    livelihood support
  organisation: Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO)
  format: In-person
  description: >-
    This interactive workshop will explore how, amid conflict, climate extremes, and economic
    shocks, risk-informed resilience in agrifood systems is essential to effective humanitarian
    response. The session will highlight lessons from FAO and partners on using agricultural
    interventions to strengthen youth agency, self-reliance, and resilience in crisis settings.
    Through keynote speakers, mixed practitioner–youth working groups, and sharing experiences
    and best practices, the workshop will explore strategies to better engage youth in agrifood
    systems prior, during and post- emergencies. It will also feed into a broader consultative
    process including the World Food Forum (WFF) Youth Assembly and FAO to ensure youth perspectives
    inform policy and decision-making.
- title: 'SparkX Dreamscape: Sparking the humanitarian for the greater good'
  organisation: Michelle Eglin, Founding President, SparkX Foundation
  format: Online
  description: >-
    This SparkX Dreamscape invites participants into the collective inner world shaping humanitarian
    action. The session explores the dynamics below the surface that both propel and sabotage
    change. Through guided reflection, symbolic imagery, and collective sense-making, participants
    attend to the unconscious forces influencing our shared humanity and the role of the humanitarian
    within it. The space privileges reflection and insight over problem-solving.
- title: >-
    Corporations & Humanitarian Contexts: Insights on corporate diplomacy & multi-stakeholder
    collaboration
  organisation: Catherine McDonald
  format: Online
  description: >-
    This workshop explores how corporations can engage in humanitarian issues, through the
    practices of corporate diplomacy and multi-stakeholder collaboration. Participants will
    be encouraged to think critically about the roles and responsibilities of corporations
    in conflict affected settings and how they can contribute as a force for good. Following
    the workshop, participants will leave with an understanding of how firms currently engage
    in humanitarian issues, their responsibilities in such settings, and a ‘toolkit’ of skills
    that can help them navigate corporate engagement in complex or conflict affected settings.
- title: 'Fighting The Emerging No-Truth Era: (Dis)Information in humanitarian contexts'
  organisation: Max Morel, ETH NADEL
  format: Online
  description: >-
    Through interactive exercises and provocative discussions, participants will learn about
    key characteristics and trends in humanitarian information environments and develop ideas
    of what humanitarian actors – and their supporters around the world – can do to limit
    potentially harmful information and promote facts.
- title: Who Cares for the Carers?  An introduction to young carers
  organisation: Caring4YoungCarers - Natasha Hunziker and Sophia Hunziker
  format: Online
  description: >-
    Did you know that around 10% of young people in Switzerland — over 180,000 — care for
    a family member?  That’s 1–2 students per classroom. These young people are called young
    carers. Young carers support a family member with a physical or mental illness, addiction,
    or disability, often silently. Most are unaware of their situation and receive little
    to no support, affecting their education, wellbeing, and future. In this interactive workshop,
    you’ll learn who young carers are, how to recognise young carers, understand why their
    invisibility is a social and humanitarian issue, and explore practical ways to support
    them.
- title: 'Building Bridges Through Cultural Activities: Connecting local and displaced communities'

As the humanitarian sector is facing significant disruption, long-standing donor commitments are weakening, crises are becoming more complex, and the implementation of International Humanitarian Law (IHL) is under growing strain, the Summit created a space for young people to reflect on how humanitarian action can remain relevant, principled, and impactful in the years to come. It moved beyond simply reaffirming principles to examine how humanitarian norms are applied in practice and explored the interaction between humanitarian, political, economic, corporate and technological actors, as well as the growing importance of community-based, refugee-led, and youth-driven responses.
